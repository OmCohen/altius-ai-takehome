## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The report covers the quarter ended 31 March 2025 for a large European buyout fund that is now substantially deployed, with paid-in capital at roughly the mid-90% range of commitments and limited remaining capital available for new investing. The quarter was characterized by modest overall portfolio value decline, selective follow-on funding into existing positions, and a meaningful partial realization through a portfolio company recapitalization/share buyback. Portfolio performance was mixed: several businesses delivered strong earnings growth and valuation uplift, while others saw earnings pressure, debt-related valuation drag, or macro-related softness. One leisure-related investment remained written off and was in the process of being transferred to lenders.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The manager highlights a weaker 2025 outlook across major European markets, shaped by elevated uncertainty, slowing investment, and trade disruption from new US tariff actions. Key themes include:

- Falling inflation and lower interest rates are expected to support consumption, but not enough to offset weaker business investment.
- US tariff escalation is expected to pressure European exports, with risks of further sector-specific tariffs and broader reciprocal measures.
- The UK is expected to post sub-1% growth in 2025, with inflation pressure from energy costs and weaker business sentiment following tax changes and trade uncertainty.
- Continental Europe is expected to see weak growth, with export-oriented economies particularly exposed to tariff and competitiveness pressures.
- Germany faces especially weak near-term growth, with delayed investment, job losses, and industrial caution, though medium-term fiscal spending could improve the outlook.
- Brazil is slowing after stronger prior growth, with high rates, persistent inflation, and weaker confidence weighing on activity.
- Denmark is expected to remain relatively resilient, supported by easing inflation and strong pharmaceutical exports, though still constrained by high rates and weak regional demand.

Sector-level and operating signals discussed in portfolio commentary include:
- Freight markets remain soft, with tariffs adding disruption.
- Luxury consumer demand in the US and Europe remains weak.
- Industrial and automation demand remains soft.
- Some businesses are facing inflationary input costs, including raw materials, energy, transport, and excise duties.
- Certain companies are using pricing actions, cost control, and operational initiatives to offset margin pressure.

### Strategy
The manager remains focused on control-oriented investing in businesses with strong market positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions. Current posture appears selective and disciplined rather than broadly risk-on, with activity concentrated in:
- follow-on investments into existing portfolio companies,
- tuck-in acquisitions at portfolio company level,
- operational value creation plans,
- selective recapitalizations and return of capital where available.

The fund made additional investments into several existing holdings during and shortly after quarter-end, while available capital for new investing has narrowed materially. The portfolio commentary suggests continued emphasis on operational improvement, M&A integration, pricing actions, cost discipline, and strategic repositioning.

### Risks & Stress
The report contains several clear stress signals across the portfolio:

- One leisure asset had previously been marked to zero and was being transferred to lenders, with a full cost write-off expected in the following quarter.
- A building materials-related business was rated in corrective action status and valued near zero, with higher debt offsetting earnings improvement.
- Several companies experienced valuation declines due to lower earnings, higher debt, weaker market conditions, or adverse FX.
- A veterinary services business faced regulatory investigation and macro-related pressure on topline growth and profitability.
- A branded spirits business faced excise duty increases, higher central costs, and delayed ability to pass through pricing.
- A logistics business continues to operate in a soft freight market disrupted by tariffs.
- A luxury goods business is facing prolonged macro headwinds and weaker consumer spending.
- A parcel logistics business delivered strong volume growth but materially lower EBITDA and margin erosion following strategic pricing decisions.
- Some software and consulting businesses reported muted markets, delayed sales, or profitability pressure from project delivery costs.
- Debt remains a valuation headwind in several assets, and the manager explicitly cites debt increases as a driver of mark-downs in multiple cases.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The portfolio was revalued as of 31 March 2025 using a methodology aligned with ASC 820 and international private equity valuation guidelines. Valuations are based primarily on applying appropriate market multiples to earnings or revenue metrics, deducting external debt, and allocating attributable equity value across the capital structure. The manager notes that portfolio trading performance is reviewed monthly by the portfolio committee.

The manager also notes:
- a group-wide responsible investment policy is in place and publicly available;
- the policy includes exclusions and critical-risk sectors requiring escalation or avoidance;
- the fund reports in line with ILPA quarterly reporting standards and subscription line best-practice guidance;
- the fund was operated in compliance with the partnership agreement during the quarter.

The fund continues to use a capital call facility as short-term working capital rather than long-term leverage, with the manager emphasizing that it is intended to reduce call frequency and support transaction execution speed.

### Performance (Censored)
Portfolio performance was broadly positive since inception but softer in the quarter. Overall fair value declined slightly quarter over quarter, while the portfolio remained valued above cost. Net returns and gross investment-level returns both declined from the prior quarter. Performance remains supported by a number of strong-performing assets with meaningful value creation, but this was offset by earnings weakness, debt-related pressure, and macro headwinds in several holdings. Realizations remain limited relative to fund size, though the quarter included a meaningful partial return from one high-performing investment.
