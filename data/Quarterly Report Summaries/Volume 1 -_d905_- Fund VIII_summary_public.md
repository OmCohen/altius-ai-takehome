## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter was marked by continued deployment into new and existing investments, including a secondary acquisition from an earlier affiliated vehicle, a majority acquisition in logistics, follow-on funding for staged investments in sports/media rights, and an increase in ownership in a nutrition business. The manager also announced several add-on acquisitions in a consumer portfolio company and signed a new co-control investment in food distribution in Latin America.

Portfolio value increased meaningfully during the quarter, driven primarily by acquisitions and positive operating performance, partly offset by some multiple compression, debt effects, and isolated weaker trading in selected assets. The portfolio remained broadly above cost overall, with fair value at roughly 115% of residual cost.

Operating commentary across the portfolio was mixed but generally constructive: several healthcare, business services, consulting, software, and consumer assets reported revenue and EBITDA growth, while a smaller number of businesses faced softer demand, inflation pressure, FX headwinds, or cyclical weakness. There were no realizations during the quarter.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report describes a slower macro backdrop across Europe and the UK, shaped by persistent inflation, rising interest rates, tighter monetary policy, and weaker external demand. Growth expectations for 2023 were subdued across major European economies, with particular weakness highlighted in energy-intensive industrial sectors and consumer-sensitive markets.

Key themes included:
- high inflation eroding purchasing power
- rising borrowing costs constraining consumption, investment, and housing activity
- easing but still relevant energy-security concerns for the coming winter
- improving supply chains and some support from public investment programs
- softer freight markets and rate normalization in logistics
- inflation pass-through opportunities in staple consumer categories
- continued FX volatility affecting reported results in internationally exposed businesses
- a softer but not collapsed US outlook, with slower growth, tighter credit conditions, and signs of household strain

Sector-level signals were mixed:
- logistics saw lower freight rates and softer volumes, though some operators maintained gross profit growth
- consumer staples showed resilience where pricing could offset input inflation
- travel and leisure remained uneven, with domestic value-oriented demand holding up better than discretionary big-ticket purchases
- healthcare and outsourced services generally remained more resilient
- software and tech-enabled services continued to show organic growth, though some end markets softened

### Strategy
The manager remained risk-on in deployment, continuing to invest actively despite the more challenging macro environment. The strategy emphasized:
- control and influential minority positions
- follow-on funding into existing platforms
- add-on acquisitions to build scale
- bilateral and proprietary sourcing where possible
- selective syndication to co-investment vehicles to manage facility usage
- focus on businesses with strong market positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions

The tone was constructive and opportunistic rather than defensive. At the same time, portfolio companies were encouraged to pursue cost savings, pricing actions, operational improvements, and disciplined M&A where market conditions warranted.

### Risks & Stress
The report highlights several stress factors across the portfolio and broader market:
- rising interest rates and tighter monetary policy
- inflation in wages, services, and input costs
- weaker consumer demand in some markets
- cyclical weakness in discretionary categories
- freight rate normalization and lower shipment volumes
- FX headwinds
- softer housing and construction-related demand in certain industrial end markets
- muted performance in some consumer-facing operating businesses
- risk of further monetary tightening if inflation remains sticky
- sovereign and refinancing risk in more indebted European economies
- housing market pressure in highly leveraged markets

At the asset level, stress was visible in a holiday/leisure business facing depressed sales, a stone-surface manufacturer experiencing a sharp valuation decline amid weaker industry demand, and a vehicle services business facing margin pressure from labor costs and acquisition integration. Some sports/media assets also faced temporary earnings pressure from direct-to-consumer ramp-up costs and a broadcaster default.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The report is unaudited and prepared in two volumes, with financial statements and capital accounts provided separately. Valuations were conducted quarterly in line with ASC 820 and international private equity valuation guidelines, primarily using earnings-based multiples adjusted for external debt and security seniority. The portfolio committee reviews trading performance monthly and incorporates valuation adjustments in subsequent quarter-end reporting.

Governance items included:
- advisory board approval for the related-party secondary acquisition between affiliated vehicles
- reporting aligned with ILPA quarterly reporting and subscription line guidance
- confirmation of compliance with the partnership agreement for the quarter
- continued use of an investor portal for document access

Operationally, the fund continued to use a capital call facility to fund intra-year needs, with annual LP drawdowns expected to repay the facility ahead of year-end. The manager emphasized that the facility is intended as short-term working capital rather than structural leverage.

### Performance (Censored)
Portfolio performance was positive overall for the quarter, with fair value increasing and the portfolio remaining above aggregate cost. Performance appears broadly in line with or modestly above underwriting at the portfolio level, though dispersion across assets is meaningful.

Several investments showed strong value creation supported by earnings growth, while others were flat to down due to weaker demand, margin pressure, or lower sector valuations. A few assets were materially below cost, but the broader portfolio continued to benefit from gains in healthcare, consulting, software, industrial distribution, and selected consumer businesses.
