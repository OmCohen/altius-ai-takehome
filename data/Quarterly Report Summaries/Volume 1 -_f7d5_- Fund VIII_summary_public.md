## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter reflected continued portfolio expansion, positive aggregate valuation movement, and limited realization activity. The fund added a new investment in food distribution and supported an existing consumer platform through multiple add-on acquisitions. Portfolio fair value increased meaningfully over the quarter, with overall value remaining above cost. Operating performance across the portfolio was mixed: several businesses delivered revenue and EBITDA growth, while a smaller set faced macro pressure, weaker demand, or margin compression. The manager also highlighted ongoing use of a capital call facility for intra-year funding and issued an October capital call to repay that facility.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report describes a still-challenging macro backdrop shaped by high interest rates, slowing external demand, and uneven inflation normalization.

Key themes included:
- High rates continuing to weigh on consumption, investment, and borrowing-sensitive sectors.
- Inflation pressures easing in some markets, but still affecting wage costs, energy costs, and consumer spending patterns.
- Slower export demand from major trading partners, particularly in Europe.
- Expectations for monetary easing beginning around mid-2024 in some developed markets, supporting a gradual recovery thereafter.
- Ongoing effects from the war in Ukraine, especially through energy markets and industrial competitiveness.
- Consumer trade-down behavior benefiting lower-cost and staple categories in some sectors.
- Freight markets remaining weak, with pricing pressure and delayed capacity exits.
- Advertising markets remaining soft.
- Housing and real estate stress in some northern European markets due to higher borrowing costs and leveraged households.

Sector-level signals discussed:
- Consumer staples and value-oriented offerings showed resilience where inflation could be passed through.
- Travel, hospitality, and tourism-linked demand was mixed but supported in some markets by post-pandemic normalization.
- Industrial and manufacturing demand softened in several areas.
- Technology and services businesses generally continued to grow, though utilization and hiring ahead of demand pressured margins in some cases.

### Strategy
The manager’s posture appears selectively risk-on. The fund continued deploying capital through both a new platform investment and add-on acquisitions, while emphasizing proprietary sourcing, bilateral processes, and sector knowledge. The stated investment criteria remain focused on businesses with strong market positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions and strategic partnerships.

Portfolio strategy themes included:
- Active buy-and-build execution.
- Continued pursuit of tuck-in acquisitions and adjacent expansion opportunities.
- Focus on operational improvement, digitalization, pricing initiatives, and synergy capture.
- In some challenged businesses, a more cautious stance with M&A deprioritized in favor of improving core operations and cost control.

### Risks & Stress
The report contains several stress indicators across the portfolio:
- Earnings pressure from foreign exchange headwinds.
- Margin pressure from inflation, wage costs, energy costs, and lower utilization.
- Freight recession and weaker logistics pricing.
- Advertising slowdown affecting revenue and EBITDA in media-related assets.
- Consumer sentiment weakness in discretionary categories.
- Slowing market growth in certain industrial and building-related end markets.
- Elevated leverage in at least one technology services asset.
- A severe valuation decline in one building materials-related investment driven by a significant earnings drop and higher debt.
- One leisure asset was rated “Early Warning,” reflecting deteriorating trends.
- One building materials asset was rated “Corrective Action,” indicating recovery risk if performance does not improve.

The manager also noted refinancing-related sensitivity indirectly through debt levels and valuation impacts from changes in leverage, though no broad default wave was described.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The report states that valuations were prepared in accordance with ASC 820 and international private equity valuation guidelines. Unquoted investments are valued primarily using appropriate market multiples applied to earnings or revenue metrics, less external debt, with allocation across the capital structure as relevant. The portfolio is reviewed monthly by the portfolio committee, with valuation adjustments reflected in subsequent quarter-end reporting.

Operational and governance points disclosed:
- The report is unaudited.
- The fund is reported in line with ILPA quarterly reporting standards and subscription line best-practice guidance.
- The manager stated the fund operated in compliance with the partnership agreement during the quarter.
- The capital call facility is described as an intra-year working capital tool rather than long-term leverage, intended to reduce call frequency and enable rapid transaction execution.

### Performance (Censored)
Portfolio performance was broadly positive for the quarter. Aggregate fair value increased and the portfolio remained marked above cost. Several investments saw valuation gains driven by stronger earnings, multiple expansion, favorable FX, or debt reduction. A smaller number of assets experienced declines due to weaker earnings, lower multiples, higher debt, or adverse FX. Overall, the portfolio appears to be performing modestly above cost, with returns supported by value creation in a number of larger holdings but offset by notable underperformance in a few challenged assets.
