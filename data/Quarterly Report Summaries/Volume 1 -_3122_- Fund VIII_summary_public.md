## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter was marked by continued deployment, several newly announced acquisitions, and limited realization activity. The portfolio remained broadly above cost on a fair value basis, but quarter-over-quarter valuation declined modestly as a number of holdings were marked down on weaker earnings, lower multiples, and FX headwinds, partly offset by gains in selected assets. The manager also highlighted additional investment activity announced after quarter-end, indicating an active pace of deployment into early 2024.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report describes a backdrop of easing inflation but still-restrictive monetary policy, with interest rates expected to remain elevated at least through mid-2024 in major developed markets. Growth expectations across Europe are modest, supported by improving real wages and public investment, but constrained by high borrowing costs and subdued external demand.

Key themes include:
- Tight monetary policy continuing to weigh on investment activity and credit demand.
- Consumer spending pressured by still-high costs for staples and, in some markets, rising mortgage-servicing burdens.
- Export-oriented economies facing weak external demand, especially from large global trading partners.
- Energy conditions improving versus prior periods, though industrial competitiveness remains challenged in some manufacturing-heavy markets.
- Inflation is easing, but the risk of stickier inflation and delayed rate cuts remains a downside scenario.
- Housing and household leverage risks remain relevant in certain markets, with higher debt-service costs constraining consumption.
- In some consumer categories, inflation has been passed through successfully, while discretionary spending remains softer.

Sector signals in the portfolio commentary suggest:
- Freight and logistics markets are stabilizing after prior weakness.
- Advertising markets remain soft, though some digital channels are recovering.
- Consumer staples are benefiting from resilient demand and inflation pass-through.
- Certain industrial and renovation-linked end markets remain weak after prior post-pandemic strength.
- Sports and media rights businesses benefit from contracted revenue visibility, though some broadcaster and distribution risks remain.

### Strategy
The manager’s posture appears risk-on but selective. The fund continues to deploy capital actively into control and minority positions across software, logistics, consumer, gaming, and services businesses, including founder-led and bilateral opportunities as well as sponsor-backed transactions. The stated investment criteria emphasize strong market position, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions.

The report also points to:
- Continued pursuit of add-on acquisitions across existing portfolio companies.
- Focus on value creation through operational improvement, cost actions, international expansion, digitalization, and M&A.
- Willingness to provide follow-on equity where needed.
- Use of a capital call facility as a short-term working capital tool to enable rapid execution and reduce call frequency.

Overall tone: actively deploying, constructive on sourcing, and focused on operational value creation, while remaining attentive to market-specific pressures.

### Risks & Stress
The report contains several stress indicators across the portfolio:
- Multiple assets experienced fair value declines driven by lower earnings, lower valuation multiples, and adverse FX movements.
- Discretionary consumer demand remains weak in some areas, particularly for big-ticket purchases.
- Advertising-related businesses continue to face slower end-market demand.
- Some businesses are dealing with partner concentration issues, platform revenue pressure, or temporary business disruption.
- One portfolio company is explicitly rated “Early Warning,” reflecting deteriorating trends.
- One portfolio company is rated “Corrective Action,” indicating recovery of investment cost is jeopardized.
- A technology services business reported elevated leverage following acquisitions.
- Certain assets are experiencing margin pressure from lower utilization, restructuring effects, or cost base built for faster growth than currently realized.
- Industrial and renovation-related demand has softened materially in some markets.
- FX headwinds affected several portfolio companies.
- The manager notes broader macro risks including recession risk, tighter-than-expected financial conditions, sovereign market stress, and geopolitical escalation affecting inflation and growth.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The report is unaudited and prepared in two volumes, with financial statements and capital accounts included separately. The manager states the fund operated in compliance with the partnership agreement during the quarter.

Valuation and governance notes include:
- Portfolio valuations are prepared in line with ASC 820 and international private equity valuation guidelines.
- Unquoted investments are valued primarily using appropriate earnings or revenue multiples, less external debt, with allocation across the capital structure as relevant.
- The portfolio committee reviews trading performance monthly, with valuation adjustments reflected in subsequent quarter-end reporting.
- Reporting is prepared in accordance with ILPA quarterly reporting standards and subscription line best-practice guidance.
- The manager disclosed use of a capital call facility as an intra-year liquidity tool rather than long-term leverage, with the facility fully repaid by quarter-end.
- Organizational updates included several senior and junior team hires, one role transition to senior advisor, and some departures.

### Performance (Censored)
The portfolio remains above cost overall on a fair value basis, indicating positive aggregate unrealized appreciation since inception. However, performance softened during the quarter as fair value declined modestly from the prior quarter. Directionally, the portfolio appears mixed: many assets remain above underwriting or value-accretive, while a smaller subset shows material underperformance and impairment risk. Realizations remain limited, so performance is still primarily driven by unrealized marks and operating progress rather than exits.
