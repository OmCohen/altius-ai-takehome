## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter was characterized by continued portfolio buildout, with several new investments closed and additional commitments signed into early 2023. The fund remained in an active deployment phase, using a subscription facility to bridge intra-year funding and drawing LP capital annually. Portfolio value declined modestly during the quarter despite new acquisitions, as negative valuation movements from multiple compression, FX, and higher debt offset earnings growth in parts of the portfolio. Operationally, portfolio performance was mixed: several businesses delivered strong revenue and EBITDA growth, while a smaller group faced consumer weakness, softer end markets, integration issues, or slower sales cycles.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report describes a difficult macro backdrop entering 2023:

- European growth is expected to stagnate, constrained by high inflation, sharply rising interest rates, weaker global demand, and ongoing energy supply uncertainty.
- Energy costs remain elevated despite some easing in natural gas prices, with pressure particularly acute for energy-intensive industries.
- The war in Eastern Europe continues to weigh on growth, trade, sanctions, and energy security.
- The UK is expected to face a deeper downturn than many European peers, driven by inflation, monetary tightening, weak real wages, and structural growth constraints.
- Across several markets, inflation is eroding consumer purchasing power and pressuring discretionary demand.
- Freight markets softened in the second half of 2022 after stronger conditions earlier in the year, with rate normalization and lower volumes in some transport segments.
- Digital advertising weakened in the second half of 2022 amid recession concerns and lower ad spending.
- Certain regulated or contracted-revenue businesses were described as more resilient, benefiting from long-term contracts, stable regulation, or essential-service demand.
- Supply chain conditions improved in some industrial and technology-related businesses, including easing chip shortages, though inflation in labor, raw materials, utilities, packaging, and transport remained a recurring theme.

### Strategy
The manager remains risk-on overall, with active deployment and multiple new investments completed or signed during and shortly after the quarter. Strategy themes include:

- Investing in market-leading businesses with strong competitive positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth.
- Pursuing both platform investments and selective add-on acquisitions.
- Continuing to back operational value creation plans, including pricing actions, cost reduction, centralization, go-to-market improvements, direct sales transitions, and targeted M&A.
- Maintaining price discipline on acquisitions in a more volatile market.
- In some challenged assets, shifting emphasis from expansion to operational stabilization and integration before resuming broader M&A activity.
- Using the capital call facility as a working-capital bridge to support rapid transaction execution and reduce call frequency.

Overall tone is constructive but selective: still deploying capital actively, while emphasizing operational execution, pricing discipline, and portfolio support in a tougher environment.

### Risks & Stress
Key risks and stress signals discussed include:

- Multiple compression and negative FX movements affecting valuations.
- Inflationary pressure across labor, utilities, raw materials, packaging, transport, and other operating costs.
- Consumer weakness affecting discretionary categories, including lower confidence, tighter financing conditions, and higher cancellation rates in some leisure-related demand.
- Integration risk in recently acquired assets, particularly where underperformance is concentrated in newly acquired operations.
- Slower deal flow, elongated sales cycles, and delayed implementation starts in some software and services businesses.
- Softening freight demand and rate normalization after unusually favorable prior market conditions.
- Advertising market weakness and client churn in parts of the media technology portfolio.
- Margin pressure from investment spending, utilization dilution from rapid hiring, and temporary negative operating leverage.
- Exposure to refinancing and debt service conditions is visible in the portfolio debt maturity schedule, though most debt appears termed out beyond 2025; one business is noted as having quarterly covenant testing.
- Some businesses are managing through temporary operational disruptions such as supply chain shortages, workforce disruption, and post-pandemic normalization effects.

### Fund Operations & Governance
Operational and governance disclosures include:

- The report is prepared in accordance with ILPA quarterly reporting standards and includes unaudited financial statements in a separate volume.
- Valuations follow ASC 820 and the International Private Equity and Venture Capital Valuation Guidelines, primarily using market multiples applied to relevant financial metrics, less external debt and adjusted across the capital structure.
- The manager states that portfolio trading is reviewed monthly by the portfolio committee, with valuation adjustments reflected in subsequent quarter-end reporting.
- The fund was reported to be in full compliance with its partnership agreement for the quarter.
- ESG reporting is included at portfolio level, with data points covering emissions, energy consumption, board composition, hiring, attrition, employee surveys, and safety metrics for certain companies.
- The manager disclosed continued use of a subscription facility as an intra-year liquidity management tool rather than structural leverage.

### Performance (Censored)
Performance was mixed in the quarter:

- The portfolio remained above cost overall, but fair value as a percentage of cost declined from the prior quarter.
- Total portfolio fair value decreased modestly during the quarter despite new acquisitions.
- Broadly, earnings growth across parts of the portfolio was offset by higher debt, FX headwinds, and lower valuation multiples.
- Several portfolio companies were performing strongly and above plan, particularly in healthcare, consulting, industrial distribution, and selected consumer staples or branded businesses.
- A smaller set of assets showed underperformance tied to discretionary consumer weakness, integration challenges, softer advertising demand, or slower enterprise sales.
- At the fund level, return metrics declined meaningfully from the prior quarter, reflecting the tougher valuation environment and the effect of increased capital drawn.
