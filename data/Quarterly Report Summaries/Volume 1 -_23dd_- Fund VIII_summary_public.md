## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter reflected continued deployment into new and follow-on investments, with portfolio fair value and fund NAV increasing versus year-end. The portfolio remained broadly diversified across consumer, healthcare, business services, technology, sports, manufacturing, energy/utilities, and gaming. The manager highlighted active investment activity during the quarter and into the following months, alongside generally constructive portfolio performance, though with mixed results across individual holdings and some stressed situations in consumer-facing and cyclical businesses.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report describes a modest improvement in the macro backdrop for 2024, supported by easing inflation, tight labor markets, and an expectation that interest rates begin to decline in the second half of the year. At the same time, growth is expected to remain subdued in several developed markets due to still-tight monetary policy, weak external demand, and elevated borrowing costs.

Key themes included:
- Euro area growth expected to improve only modestly, with stronger private consumption partly offset by weak trade demand and restrictive monetary policy in the first half.
- The UK outlook improving gradually as inflation eases and real wages strengthen, but activity remains constrained by high household costs, mortgage resets, and election-related uncertainty.
- Manufacturing-heavy economies were described as weaker than tourism- and services-oriented markets.
- Export-oriented economies remain exposed to soft demand from major trading partners.
- Geopolitical risks include the ongoing war in Eastern Europe, long-term sanctions-related decoupling, and broader conflict-related inflation risk through energy markets.
- Regulatory and political developments may affect trade frictions, green policy ambition, and cross-border operating conditions.

Sector-level signals discussed in portfolio commentary included:
- Freight markets showing signs of stabilization.
- Advertising markets gradually improving after a slowdown, with industry forecasts turning more positive.
- Consumer demand remaining uneven, especially for discretionary big-ticket purchases and luxury goods.
- Staple categories benefiting from inflationary environments where pricing can be passed through.
- Some support for logistics and transport pricing from supply chain disruption in global shipping lanes.

### Strategy
The manager’s posture appears risk-on and actively deploying. The fund continued to make new platform investments, fund follow-ons, and pursue transactions into the second quarter. The stated investment criteria emphasize businesses with strong market positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions and operational improvement.

The report also indicates:
- Willingness to pursue both majority and minority structures.
- Use of sector knowledge, long-term tracking, and relationship-driven sourcing to secure transactions.
- Continued support for portfolio companies through add-on acquisitions, digitalization, international expansion, and operational initiatives.
- Ongoing use of a capital call facility to enable rapid execution and reduce call frequency.

### Risks & Stress
The report identifies a number of stress signals across the portfolio and broader environment:
- Elevated borrowing costs and tight monetary policy continue to pressure growth and investment activity.
- Consumer-facing businesses remain exposed to weak discretionary spending, especially in big-ticket categories and luxury.
- Some businesses are facing soft end-market demand, supply chain normalization effects, or temporary channel disruption.
- Certain holdings experienced earnings pressure from lower volumes, customer disputes over pricing, delayed deliveries, or slower market demand.
- One portfolio company was classified as “Early Warning,” indicating deteriorating trends that could jeopardize recovery if not corrected.
- One portfolio company was classified as “Corrective Action,” indicating recovery is not adequately protected by the company’s financial condition.
- Several valuation changes were affected by higher debt, lower earnings, or negative FX movements.
- The report also notes risks from broadcaster default, weak market demand in certain healthcare services, and social-media-related brand disruption.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The report is unaudited and prepared in two volumes, with financial statements and capital accounts included separately. The manager states the fund operated in full compliance with the partnership agreement during the quarter.

Valuation and governance points disclosed include:
- Portfolio revaluation was performed at quarter-end.
- Fair value methodology follows ASC 820 and international private equity valuation guidelines.
- Unquoted investments are generally valued using appropriate earnings or revenue multiples, less external debt, with value then allocated across securities.
- Portfolio company performance is reviewed monthly by the portfolio committee, with valuation adjustments reflected in subsequent quarter-end reporting.
- Reporting is prepared in accordance with ILPA quarterly reporting standards and subscription line guidance.
- The manager disclosed organizational changes, including several new hires and a senior leadership transition to an honorary role.

The report also discusses the capital call facility as an intra-year working capital tool rather than long-term leverage, emphasizing its role in speeding transaction execution and reducing the number of capital calls.

### Performance (Censored)
Portfolio performance was positive overall during the quarter. Fair value increased meaningfully from year-end, and the portfolio remained valued above cost. The fund’s NAV also rose quarter over quarter. Directionally, the portfolio appears to be tracking above cost and generating moderate positive unrealized appreciation overall, though performance is uneven across holdings.

The report indicates:
- Broad portfolio fair value increased during the quarter.
- Several holdings benefited from higher valuation multiples, earnings growth, debt reduction, or favorable FX.
- A smaller number of holdings saw declines due to weaker earnings, higher debt, or adverse FX.
- A few investments remain materially challenged, including one with severe value erosion relative to cost.
