## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter reflected continued rapid portfolio buildout, with the fund increasing from 12 to 14 investments and total invested capital rising to about €8.7bn. Portfolio fair value increased meaningfully during the period, driven primarily by new acquisitions and positive revaluations across several existing holdings. The manager highlighted strong operating performance across much of the portfolio, with many businesses showing revenue and earnings growth despite ongoing pandemic effects, supply-chain disruption, and inflationary pressures. No realizations occurred during the quarter.

The period narrative also emphasized a large near-term pipeline and committed capital base, with several signed or announced transactions expected to consume additional capital. The fund continued to rely on a capital call facility to fund intra-year investment activity and expected a sizable capital call after quarter-end to partially repay that facility.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report described a mixed macro backdrop. Economic recovery from the pandemic continued, but growth slowed as supply-chain disruption, semiconductor shortages, shipping constraints, and sharply higher energy prices weighed on activity. Inflation was elevated across major markets, with energy and food costs identified as key drivers. The invasion of a neighboring Eastern European country was described as a major external shock for Europe, expected to reduce growth, worsen inflation, and increase stagflation and recession risks, particularly if gas supplies were disrupted.

In Europe, the manager noted pressure on manufacturing, autos, food production, and other energy- or input-sensitive sectors. In the UK, higher commodity prices, tax increases, post-separation trade frictions, and Asia-related supply disruptions were expected to weaken consumption and business investment. In the US, the outlook assumed multiple policy rate increases, with higher borrowing costs expected to moderate consumer spending, fixed investment, and housing activity, while inflation and geopolitical instability remained downside risks.

Sector commentary in the portfolio pointed to several recurring themes:
- Recovery in healthcare procedure volumes as pandemic disruption eased.
- Strong demand in domestic leisure and travel substitution.
- Continued growth in digital advertising and technology-enabled services.
- Ability in some consumer businesses to pass through raw material inflation via pricing.
- Ongoing resilience in non-discretionary, mission-critical industrial and aftermarket demand.

### Strategy
The manager’s posture appeared broadly risk-on, with active deployment across a wide range of sectors and multiple new signed transactions in the quarter and shortly thereafter. The investment approach remained focused on businesses with strong market positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions.

Portfolio construction showed emphasis on healthcare, business services, technology, consumer, sports/media rights, and industrial distribution. The manager repeatedly framed investments as platform opportunities with scope for organic growth, geographic expansion, product expansion, pricing initiatives, digital capability enhancement, and M&A. Several portfolio companies were pursuing add-ons or tuck-in acquisitions, and the manager continued to support inorganic growth where strategically accretive.

### Risks & Stress
The report highlighted several macro and operating risks:
- Inflation in energy, food, packaging, and raw materials.
- Supply-chain disruption and component shortages.
- Geopolitical shock and commodity price volatility.
- Risk of stagflation and recession, especially in Europe.
- Continued uncertainty from pandemic variants and related restrictions.
- Labor availability issues in certain service businesses.
- Exposure of some sectors to reduced purchasing power and weaker consumer demand.

At the asset level, stress signals were limited overall, but there were isolated pressures from foreign exchange movements, temporary procedure disruption from pandemic waves, and cost inflation. One newly acquired investment was marked below cost at quarter-end, and another consumer asset showed a slight mark-down. The report also noted uncertainty in fair value determination given the broader macro environment.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The report was unaudited and prepared in accordance with recognized quarterly reporting and subscription line disclosure standards. Valuations were stated to follow fair value guidance and international private equity valuation guidelines, primarily using market multiples applied to earnings or revenue metrics, less external debt and adjusted for security structure.

The manager noted monthly portfolio review by its internal portfolio committee, with valuation adjustments incorporated in quarter-end reporting as needed. The fund was reported to be in compliance with its governing agreement during the quarter.

On ESG and responsible investment, the report included portfolio ESG performance data and company-level commentary. Examples included employee engagement initiatives, training, ethics and integrity policies, environmental compliance procedures, greenhouse gas measurement efforts, workplace safety metrics, diversity indicators, and circular-economy benefits in certain businesses.

Operationally, the manager continued to use a capital call facility as an intra-year working capital tool rather than long-term leverage, emphasizing speed of execution and reduced frequency of LP drawdowns.

### Performance (Censored)
Portfolio performance was broadly positive. The residual portfolio was valued above cost at quarter-end, and fair value as a percentage of cost increased from the prior quarter. Most investments were marked at or above cost, with several showing strong early uplifts. Operating commentary across the portfolio was generally favorable, with many businesses reporting revenue and EBITDA growth, margin resilience, backlog support, or demand recovery. A small number of assets were flat to modestly below cost.
