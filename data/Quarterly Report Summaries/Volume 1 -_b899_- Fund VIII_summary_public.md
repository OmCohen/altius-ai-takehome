## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter was marked by a sharp increase in deployment, with multiple new platform investments, follow-on funding into existing positions, and additional tranche funding for previously committed assets. Portfolio fair value and net asset value both increased meaningfully over the quarter, supported by new investments and selective valuation gains across several holdings. The portfolio remains broadly diversified across business services, software, healthcare, consumer, logistics, media rights, and industrial sectors.

The manager reported generally stable to improving operating performance across much of the portfolio, though results were mixed by company and sector. Areas of strength included software, consulting, selected healthcare services, logistics, and certain consumer and media-related businesses. Pressure remained in some consumer discretionary, industrial, and carve-out situations. The fund is now substantially drawn, with limited capital remaining available for new investing relative to total commitments.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report describes a modestly improving macro backdrop in Europe in 2024 after a difficult 2023. Key themes include easing inflation, stronger wage growth, improving real incomes, and the beginning of monetary easing in parts of developed markets, which is expected to support financing conditions and domestic demand.

Across major European economies, growth expectations remain positive but subdued, with recovery supported by consumption, EU-funded investment, and gradually lower borrowing costs. The UK is described as recovering from recessionary conditions but still constrained by weak productivity, trade frictions, and elevated household cost burdens. Germany is improving from stagnation but remains structurally challenged. Southern Europe is benefiting from tourism, wage growth, and public investment support.

The report also highlights ongoing downside risks from geopolitical tensions, elections, trade policy uncertainty, sticky inflation, and delayed monetary loosening. In sector commentary, freight markets remain soft, advertising markets are emerging from slowdown, and some consumer categories continue to feel pressure from discretionary spending weakness. FX also affected certain portfolio marks and operating results.

### Strategy
The manager remains actively deploying and appears risk-on, with several new investments completed during the quarter and shortly thereafter, including software, digital consumer, restaurants, gaming, and continuation-style transactions. The approach emphasizes sourcing through long-term sector tracking, local market relationships, bilateral or limited-process execution, and partnering with founders, management teams, and co-investors.

The stated investment criteria continue to focus on businesses with strong market positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions. The portfolio activity also reflects continued willingness to fund follow-ons, structured tranches, and sponsor-to-sponsor or affiliated transactions where governance approvals are obtained.

### Risks & Stress
The report identifies several stress signals across the portfolio. Consumer discretionary demand remains weak in some areas, especially for big-ticket purchases. Certain businesses are facing slower-than-budgeted revenue growth, margin pressure, or delayed recovery due to macro softness. Freight pricing recovery has been slower than expected. Advertising-related businesses have been affected by platform changes and prior market slowdown. Some industrial and distribution businesses are seeing weaker demand as supply chains normalize.

At the asset level, notable stress indicators include earnings declines, debt increases, and one investment marked as requiring corrective action with value reduced to a minimal level. Another holding is classified as early warning. The report also references regulatory investigation pressure in one healthcare-related business, softer occupancy or sales dynamics in selected consumer-facing assets, and carve-out execution challenges in at least one business. Elevated interest rates and debt burdens remain relevant to valuation and operating performance.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The report is unaudited and prepared in two volumes, with financial statements and capital accounts provided separately. The manager states the fund operated in compliance with its governing agreement during the quarter. Reporting is prepared in accordance with industry quarterly reporting standards and guidance on subscription line usage.

Valuations are performed quarterly in line with ASC 820 and international private equity valuation guidelines, primarily using appropriate trading multiples applied to earnings or revenue metrics, less external debt, with allocation across securities in the capital structure. The portfolio is also reviewed monthly by the internal portfolio committee.

The fund uses a capital call facility as an intra-year working capital tool to reduce call frequency and facilitate rapid transaction execution. The manager emphasizes that it is not intended as long-term leverage. The report also includes organizational updates on investment team hires and departures.

### Performance (Censored)
Portfolio performance was positive in the quarter. Fair value increased, and the portfolio overall remained above cost. The fund’s value and investment-level return metrics improved modestly versus the prior quarter. Most holdings are categorized as performing satisfactorily, with gains in a number of investments offset by weakness in a smaller subset. Overall, the portfolio appears broadly ahead of cost and generating moderate unrealized appreciation, though performance dispersion across assets is meaningful.
