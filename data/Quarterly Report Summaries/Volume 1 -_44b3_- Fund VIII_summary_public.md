## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter was defined by heavy deployment and rapid portfolio expansion. The fund increased total investments from 17 to 24 and raised invested capital to €11.6bn, with paid-in capital reaching 32.3% of commitments. Net asset value rose materially quarter over quarter, driven primarily by capital calls and new acquisitions, with no realizations or distributions during the period.

Portfolio valuation increased to €13.5bn, or 116% of cost. Most existing assets showed positive fair value movement, supported by earnings growth, favorable debt movements, selective multiple expansion, and in some cases FX tailwinds. A few assets experienced pressure from weaker earnings, softer multiples, inflation, integration issues, or adverse FX.

The manager remained highly active in signing and closing new transactions across healthcare, gaming, sports rights, industrial technology, IT services, nutrition, building products, and vehicle services. The tone of the report is constructive on portfolio company operating performance overall, while acknowledging a more difficult macro backdrop, especially in Europe and in consumer-sensitive areas.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report highlights a deteriorating macro backdrop in Europe heading into winter 2022/23, centered on energy shortages, elevated gas and power prices, and recession risk. The disruption of Russian gas supply is expected to create an energy crunch, with the severity dependent on weather, demand reduction, gas sharing, and substitution away from gas. Energy-intensive sectors such as metals and chemicals are identified as particularly exposed.

Across major developed markets, the manager notes slowing growth, elevated inflation, and tighter monetary conditions. In the UK, falling real wages, inflation, monetary tightening, supply-chain disruption, and trade frictions are expected to weigh on consumption, investment, and exports. In continental Europe, weaker external demand and high energy costs are expected to pressure growth. Some markets are viewed as relatively more resilient due to lower gas dependence or stronger fiscal positions, but still exposed to inflation and weaker regional demand.

At the sector level, the report points to:
- Softening freight markets and rate normalization after unusually favorable conditions earlier in the year.
- Continued digital advertising growth over the long term, but near-term spending delays and macro-related headwinds.
- Inflation pressure across labor, utilities, transportation, packaging, grains, glass, and other inputs.
- Recovery in certain post-Covid channels such as on-trade consumption, in-person medical activity, and land-based leisure/gaming.
- Resilient demand in staple and essential product categories.
- Ongoing supply-chain disruption in selected businesses.

FX was also a meaningful factor, with the euro weakening materially against the US dollar by quarter-end.

### Strategy
The manager is in a clear risk-on deployment phase. The fund continued to execute a broad buyout strategy focused on businesses with strong market positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions and operational improvement.

Deployment during the quarter was aggressive, with multiple new platform investments and follow-on capital. The manager also continues to emphasize:
- Buy-and-build and add-on M&A where attractive.
- Operational value creation through pricing, cost control, productivity, organizational build-out, and commercial initiatives.
- International expansion and new market entry.
- Selective investment in technology, digital capabilities, and product development.
- Patience and discipline on acquisition pricing in areas where market conditions have softened.

The portfolio commentary suggests continued conviction in long-term growth themes across healthcare, technology-enabled services, business services, consumer staples, and rights-based assets, while being more cautious in cyclical and consumer-exposed segments.

### Risks & Stress
The report identifies several stress factors across the portfolio:
- Inflation in labor, utilities, transportation, packaging, raw materials, and energy.
- Consumer weakness affecting discretionary spending and financing-sensitive purchases.
- Supply-chain delays and technical issues affecting sales conversion and operations.
- Integration challenges following rapid acquisition activity in certain assets.
- Softening freight demand and pricing normalization in logistics.
- Slower client decision-making and elongated sales cycles in software and services.
- FX headwinds in internationally exposed businesses.
- Margin pressure from investment spending, utilization dilution from hiring, and delayed pass-through of cost inflation.

One leisure-oriented asset is notably below cost following underperformance tied to consumer pressure, integration issues, and inflation. Other isolated assets saw modest valuation pressure from weaker earnings, lower multiples, or debt effects. The report also notes elevated macro uncertainty around recession risk and energy availability in Europe.

At the fund level, there were no realizations, implying continued reliance on unrealized value growth rather than cash returns. The fund also makes extensive use of a capital call facility for intra-year funding, though the manager emphasizes that this is intended as short-term working capital rather than structural leverage.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The report states that the fund remained in compliance with its partnership agreement during the quarter. Reporting is prepared in accordance with ILPA quarterly reporting standards and includes unaudited financial statements in a separate volume.

Valuations are prepared under ASC 820 and the international private equity valuation guidelines. The methodology is based primarily on applying appropriate valuation multiples to operating metrics such as EBITDA, EBITA, earnings, or revenue, then deducting external debt and allocating value across the capital structure. The portfolio is reviewed monthly by the portfolio committee, with quarter-end adjustments reflected in reported fair values.

The manager also disclosed organizational updates, including multiple senior and junior hires across operations, investment, technology, healthcare, capital markets, and regional teams, as well as selected departures and a retirement.

ESG reporting is included in the appendices, with portfolio-level data covering emissions, renewable energy consumption, board diversity, workplace injuries, hiring, and employee engagement for selected companies.

### Performance (Censored)
Portfolio performance was broadly positive in the quarter. Fair value increased meaningfully and the portfolio as a whole remained above cost. Most assets were described as performing in line with or above plan, supported by revenue growth, earnings growth, pricing actions, backlog, market share gains, or strategic progress.

A number of assets showed especially strong momentum in healthcare, consulting, logistics, industrial distribution, and selected consumer and technology businesses. Several newly acquired assets were held near cost given recent closing dates. A smaller number of assets experienced underperformance or margin pressure, particularly where consumer demand softened, integration proved difficult, or macro conditions affected volumes and pricing.

Overall, the portfolio appears to be tracking favorably, though with wider dispersion emerging across sectors and business models as macro conditions become more challenging.
