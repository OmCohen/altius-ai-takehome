## Public Summary

### General Summary (Public)
The quarter reflected continued portfolio expansion, modest NAV growth, and generally resilient operating performance across a diversified buyout portfolio, despite a more difficult macro backdrop. The fund added a new investment in sports-related commercial rights during the quarter and agreed additional transactions, including one expected to close in the following quarter and one completed after quarter-end through an inter-fund transfer approved by advisory boards.

At quarter-end, the fund had drawn a little over half of commitments and held a portfolio valued above cost. Portfolio fair value increased during the quarter, driven primarily by earnings growth and multiple expansion, partly offset by debt, FX, and a few company-specific markdowns. There were no realizations during the quarter, though the fund made a small income distribution.

The manager’s portfolio commentary emphasized operational execution, pricing actions, selective M&A, integration work, and cost control. Performance was mixed by asset, with many companies showing growth or stabilization, while a smaller group faced softer demand, margin pressure, integration issues, or macro-related volume weakness.

### Market & Macroeconomic Environment
The report describes a macro environment that remained challenging but somewhat improved relative to prior expectations. In Europe, consumer sentiment recovered from post-war lows, helped by mild weather, high gas storage, and lower energy prices. Even so, growth expectations for 2023 remained subdued, with inflation still pressuring purchasing power, monetary policy tightening across Europe, and external demand softening as global growth slowed.

The commentary highlights:
- sharply higher short-term interest rates across major markets;
- persistent inflation, though some companies were able to pass through price increases;
- easing supply-chain frictions in parts of the economy;
- continued uncertainty tied to the war in Ukraine and sanctions effects;
- a rebound in demand from China as zero-COVID policies ended;
- weaker consumer demand in some end markets;
- normalization in freight pricing and volumes in parts of logistics;
- recessionary conditions in advertising spend through late 2022 and into the first half of 2023;
- selective resilience in staple consumer categories and contractual media-rights businesses.

The UK was described as particularly weak versus European peers, with inflation, monetary tightening, and structural growth constraints weighing on activity. Across Europe more broadly, the report points to slower growth, higher borrowing costs, and lingering energy-supply risk into the next winter, though with less severe recession risk than previously feared.

### Strategy
The manager remains in active deployment mode rather than defensive retrenchment. The fund continued to invest during the quarter, signed additional transactions, and maintained a sizable committed pipeline. The stated investment criteria focus on businesses with strong market positions, credible business plans, and opportunities for cash flow and profit growth, including through acquisitions.

Portfolio strategy appears centered on:
- operational value creation;
- selective add-on M&A;
- pricing and margin management;
- digitalization and technology investment;
- international expansion and go-direct commercial strategies;
- integration of recent acquisitions;
- disciplined use of a capital call facility to fund intra-year needs and enable rapid transaction execution.

The tone is constructive but disciplined: the manager is still deploying capital and pursuing growth initiatives, while also emphasizing selectivity, cost actions, and operational improvement where macro conditions have softened.

### Risks & Stress
The report identifies several stress factors across the portfolio:
- inflation in labor, utilities, raw materials, transport, and other input costs;
- FX headwinds affecting reported results and valuations;
- softer consumer demand in discretionary categories;
- normalization in freight rates and volumes;
- slower transaction activity affecting advisory and project-based businesses;
- integration challenges at recently acquired assets;
- margin pressure from cost inflation and investment spending;
- delayed customer negotiations or elongated sales cycles in some businesses;
- weaker advertising spending and broader recession concerns in media-related markets.

Asset-level stress signals discussed in aggregate include:
- underperformance concentrated in newly acquired sites within a leisure platform;
- increased cancellation rates and financing-related pressure in consumer-facing sales;
- lower utilization following rapid hiring in a professional services business;
- delayed implementation starts and weaker new-license deal flow in a software business;
- production issues and lower operating efficiency in a manufacturing business;
- startup losses and staff cost overspend in a gaming business;
- working-capital absorption tied to rapid growth in an industrial technology business.

Debt maturity data indicate that most portfolio debt sits beyond 2025, though there are some nearer-term maturities in 2023–2025. One software business specifically notes quarterly covenant testing, while another holding structure raised substantial debt at acquisition. The manager also notes that the fund uses a subscription facility for intra-year liquidity, but frames it as working capital timing rather than structural leverage.

### Fund Operations & Governance
The report is unaudited and prepared for the quarter ended 31 March 2023. The manager states that valuations are prepared in accordance with ASC 820 and the international private equity valuation guidelines. Valuation methodology is based on applying appropriate market multiples to indicators such as EBITDA, EBITA, PAT, or revenue, then deducting external debt and allocating value across the capital structure.

The portfolio is reviewed monthly by the portfolio committee, with valuation adjustments reflected in quarter-end reporting as needed. The report also states that the fund operated in full compliance with its partnership agreement during the quarter.

Operationally, the manager highlights:
- continued use of an investor reporting portal;
- reporting aligned with ILPA quarterly reporting standards and subscription line guidance;
- additions to the investment and operations teams during the period.

### Performance (Censored)
Portfolio performance was broadly positive in the quarter. The portfolio remained valued above cost, and fair value increased quarter over quarter. The main drivers were earnings growth and multiple expansion, partially offset by debt effects, FX, and a handful of markdowns.

Overall portfolio performance appears mixed but favorable:
- many assets are performing in line with or above plan, supported by pricing, volume growth, backlog, recurring revenue, or operational initiatives;
- several businesses are benefiting from strong demand, market share gains, or successful inflation pass-through;
- a smaller subset is below underwriting or facing temporary pressure from integration issues, softer end markets, utilization declines, or cost inflation.

The fund’s overall value metrics suggest the portfolio is still in an early-to-mid hold phase, with limited realizations to date and most value remaining unrealized.
